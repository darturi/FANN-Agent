## Question, scope and claims

Can a fresh agent episode produce an executable stopping policy that preserves measured recall and reduces complete-request latency on MoReVec Reviews Small, compared with its unchanged native640 parent and a tuned native baseline?

Use the preserved original full HNSW graph, 247,286 rows, 768-dimensional cosine vectors, k=10, iterative scan off, serial persistent readers, and the published filters total_votes >= 743/527/310/192/108/32. Unfiltered is a separate control. Primary workload weighting is equal across the six scalar filters; report all seven separately. No predicate is silently omitted because it is difficult. A runtime failure is a failed/incomplete qualification; narrowing scope requires a versioned protocol amendment before validation/test and a qualified runtime, not a post-result exclusion.

The primary recall claim is preservation on the fixed held-out benchmark workload. We do **not** claim population mean-recall noninferiority within 0.25 percentage points: 275 independent groups cannot establish that worst-case guarantee merely by exhibiting zero losses. Report any future-query inference separately with its explicit sampling assumptions.

## Fresh agent episode

Use Anthropic model identifier `claude-sonnet-5-5`, global inference, standard-only service; record the exact returned model/version. Model availability is checked without sending development evidence before dispatch. Do not silently change providers/models; unavailable model means a recorded pre-execution method amendment. Reconcile existing usage locally before dispatch, reserve at most $15 for the complete episode, and log every attempt, token category, cost and uncertain charge. These checks do not spend money during runtime qualification.

At most three rounds, three policies per round, nine distinct proposals total and 6,000 output tokens per round. Supply only allowlisted development evidence, published literature context, feature semantics and measured serving costs. Do not seed the new invocation with validation/test outcomes or the old policy as a required answer. Record manual edits as separate proposals within the same nine-policy cap. A native fallback or abstention is valid; no additional model calls are made to rescue a failed bounded episode.

Executable policies may use predicate identity, checkpoints 16/32/64, qualifying count, tenth eligible distance, pending-frontier minus tenth distance, and top-ten turnover since the previous checkpoint. Missing/insufficient features imply continue. Stop only with ten valid eligible candidates. Use at most three checkpoint branches per predicate and at most four threshold comparisons per branch; no IDs, exact labels, future traversal, oracle information or arbitrary code. The first satisfied branch stops. Otherwise resume native640 unchanged. A compiler/interpreter and live-feature parity check is mandatory before any proposed policy is timed.

Measure every candidate on development and screen invalid/recall-failing candidates before validation. Use two mirrored complete-request timing epochs with fresh A/B reader labels per candidate, ten development warmups per reader, grouped query pairing, and the same endpoint as the final test. Work savings are diagnostic, never a substitute for serving time. The validation pool may contain at most nine agent proposals, five native settings, three fixed rules and six turnover rules; fixed historical comparators remain separately reported.

A quality-eligible candidate must have valid unique eligible complete outputs, native mean recall at least .95 in every condition, candidate mean recall at least .95 in every condition, paired mean difference at least -.0025, and zero query-level recall losses relative to native640 in every condition. Thus the zero-loss requirement is stronger than the finite-set mean margin. If native itself falls below the adequacy floor, report the condition and fail the combined objective; do not drop it. Require the corresponding zero-loss and mean-margin checks against selected tuned native for the stronger practical comparison.

Choose the lowest equal-filter complete-request mean among eligible candidates within each family. Differences within 1% of the minimum are ties: native chooses larger ef; fixed chooses later checkpoint; turnover chooses later checkpoint then lower turnover limit; agent chooses fewer comparisons/clauses then lexicographically smaller canonical policy SHA256. Validation timing uses four mirrored epochs; no significance claim is made from the selection timings. If no agent candidate qualifies, retain native640 and report negative selection without presenting fallback as agent success. Freeze the selected candidates before test.

## Recall scoring and inference

Recompute exact reference on the actual imported SQL representation on development. For each request force a sequential exact cosine sort with `FETCH FIRST 10 ROWS WITH TIES`; preserve self matches and source IDs. Validate unique returned IDs, filter eligibility, finite cosine distances and correct result count. Ground-truth self/tie and canonical-to-source-ID mappings must be qualified before reuse of published labels. Score returned eligible IDs at/below the exact kth boundary with absolute distance tolerance 1e-6, counting each ID once. Record boundary ties separately. Requests with fewer than k eligible records require exactly min(k, eligible_count) outputs and that denominator; an empty eligible population has recall 1 only with an empty result. Incomplete, duplicate, ineligible or nonfinite outputs fail candidate eligibility, even if an aggregate score would be high.

Report per-condition native/candidate recall, paired difference, query-level losing groups and magnitudes, missing/invalid outputs, exact-tie diagnostics, and the overall vector-group count. Final preservation requires zero observed losses for every test group in each of the seven conditions against the declared reference. The .0025 margin is recorded consistently in recall units, but is not advertised as a population confidence result.

Report exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper bounds on loss probability per condition at alpha=.05; also simultaneous per-condition bounds using alpha=.05/7 and an any-condition-loss group indicator at alpha=.05. With zero any-condition losses among 275 groups the bound is `1-.05^(1/275)`, approximately 1.0834%. These calculations assume independent comparable query draws; the fixed published benchmark set and historical availability process do not themselves guarantee that sampling model. Repetitions and predicates are not additional independent groups. Do not use a zero-width bootstrap recall interval to claim universal safety.

## Latency experiment and controls

The primary endpoint is the arithmetic mean complete-request time on an already open reader: SQL binding/dispatch, feature collection, policy evaluation, ANN and continuation, fetching and decoding. Keep setup, connection, lookup build/attach, warmup and close outside that request timer and report each separately. Preserve output validation for every timed request; perform heavy validation outside the timed interval and compare output identities to preverified quality records.

Run five independent compatible Torch allocations, each with four epochs, seven families and two identically coded A/B reader labels per family. Each reader/filter receives ten development warmup queries, followed by the full test group set; validation/test queries are never used for setup tuning. One reader per family/filter/label persists for its scheduled requests. Use 14-label Williams rows, rotating the base row [0,1,13,2,12,...,7] by allocation/epoch/group position, with mirrored epochs and mirrored paired query orders. Rotate filter order. Schedule seed: `reviews-clean-confirmatory-timing-v1`. Export the full executable schedule and its hash before test; maintain each query's cross-arm pairing and record reader identity and predecessor/order.

For each family/filter, identical-code A/B controls pass only when their paired hierarchical 90% two-sided mean-ratio interval lies wholly within [.98,1.02]. Every control relevant to a comparison must pass; pooling away a failing filter is not allowed. These equivalence checks form an intersection requirement, not a search for a favorable label. A failure makes affected performance contrasts inconclusive. Never subtract a control gap from treatment timing.

Analyze 20,000 paired hierarchical bootstrap draws, seed20261110: resample allocations then vector groups; preserve all filter, family, label and epoch observations for each sampled group. Report the ratio of equal-filter arithmetic means, absolute milliseconds, percentage reduction `100*(1-ratio)` and speedup `1/ratio`, with median and p95 secondary. Show every allocation's effect and node/CPU, and explicitly limit inference from five allocations, especially if all use one node.

The two primary latency contrasts are new agent/native640 and new agent/tuned-native. Each passes the practical target only if its one-sided 97.5% upper ratio bound is <=.95, controlling the two-comparison family at .05 by Bonferroni. Combined headline success requires quality plus both contrasts and their relevant controls. Passing only native640 supports the narrower stopping-intervention claim. Secondary scalar-filter contrasts use Holm across the twelve agent/native comparisons; unfiltered and other baseline comparisons are descriptive unless separately declared.

Five allocations/four epochs is a fixed feasible design, not a promise of sufficient statistical power. Report inconclusive intervals honestly. Before final test freeze, development evidence supplies precision projections but cannot change this count opportunistically; any justified redesign is a versioned amendment before validation/test. Do not accumulate additional allocations until success. Replace at most two infrastructure-invalid allocations with unchanged code; never replace a valid negative or statistically imbalanced allocation. Publish every invalid allocation and reason. Query omissions invalidate the allocation rather than silently reducing the cohort.
